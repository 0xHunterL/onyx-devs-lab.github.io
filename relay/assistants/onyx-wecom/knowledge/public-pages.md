## Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) | Onyx Devs Lab / 1
## Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) | Onyx Devs Lab
https://hk.onyxdevslab.com/en/forward-deployed-engineering/
Forward Deployed Engineering for complex AI transformation: field diagnosis, embedded implementation, deployment, and measurable validation in one delivery loop.
Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) combines discovery, software engineering, deployment, and outcome validation. It is designed for important operating problems that cannot be specified accurately from outside the workflow.
When FDE fits: FDE is useful when the problem changes as the team learns from real operations.
- Ambiguous requirements: Users can describe pain, but the correct intervention is not yet known.
- Complex environment: Data, policy, legacy systems, and human decisions interact in ways a brief cannot capture.
- Outcome ownership: Success must be demonstrated in an operating metric, not only in software delivery.
How FDE differs: FDE closes gaps that appear between advice, specification, engineering, and adoption.
- Versus consulting: The same team that diagnoses the problem also implements and tests the intervention.
- Versus outsourcing: The unit of success is an operating outcome, not conformance to a frozen feature list.
- Versus staff augmentation: The team owns a defined problem and evidence loop rather than supplying generic capacity.
Q: What does FDE stand for?
A: In this context, FDE means Forward Deployed Engineering: engineers work close to the customer’s real operating environment and connect discovery, implementation, and validation.
Q: Is FDE only for large enterprises?
A: No. It fits any organisation facing a valuable but underspecified operational problem, provided decision-makers and operators can participate in the evidence loop.
Q: How long is an FDE engagement?
A: Length depends on the workflow and validation window. A first cycle commonly includes diagnosis, a bounded intervention, and enough operating time to evaluate agreed metrics.

Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) is a delivery model in which engineers work close to a customer’s real operating environment and connect problem discovery, software implementation, deployment, and outcome validation in one continuous loop.
The core idea: Traditional delivery often separates diagnosis, specification, implementation, and adoption across different teams. FDE keeps those activities close enough that field evidence can change the solution.
- Discover in context: Engineers observe how work is actually performed, including exceptions and informal handoffs.
- Build with operators: Domain experts and engineers test assumptions against real cases rather than a frozen brief.
- Validate the change: The team compares the agreed baseline with post-intervention evidence and inspects unintended effects.
When not to use FDE: FDE is not automatically better for every software project.
- Stable commodity need: A standard product is usually preferable when requirements and workflows are already well understood.
- No access to operators: FDE cannot work well when the team cannot observe the process or receive timely evidence.
- No measurable boundary: The engagement needs an agreed problem, responsible owner, and a way to judge whether the intervention helped.
Q: Is FDE the same as embedded engineering?
A: They overlap, but FDE usually adds explicit ownership of discovery, implementation, and validation around a customer problem.
Q: Is FDE the same as professional services?
A: FDE may be delivered as a professional service, but its defining feature is the tight field evidence and engineering loop, not the commercial contract type.
Q: Why is FDE relevant to enterprise AI?
A: AI requirements often depend on real data, exceptions, permissions, user judgement, and model failure modes. These are difficult to specify accurately without testing inside the operating context.

## FDE 前线部署工程｜企业 AI 落地｜Onyx Devs Lab

FDE 前线部署工程把现场诊断、嵌入式开发、部署与量化验证放进同一交付循环。
FDE（前线部署工程）把需求发现、软件工程、部署和效果验证连接起来，适合无法在项目开始前完整定义的复杂企业 AI 问题。
什么场景适合 FDE: 当正确方案必须从真实业务运行中学习时，FDE 比冻结需求更合适。
- 需求仍然模糊: 团队知道痛点，但还无法准确描述正确的系统干预。
- 环境复杂: 数据、政策、旧系统和人工判断相互影响。
- 需要结果责任: 成功必须反映在运营指标，而不只是软件功能完成。
FDE 与其他模式的区别: FDE 缩短咨询、规格、工程和采用之间的反馈距离。
- 不同于传统咨询: 诊断问题的团队同时实施并验证干预。
- 不同于项目外包: 成功单位是可验证的运营变化，而非冻结功能清单。
- 不同于人力外派: 团队负责一个有边界的问题和证据闭环。
Q: FDE 是什么意思？
A: FDE 是 Forward Deployed Engineering，工程师贴近客户的真实运营环境，把发现、实施和验证连接起来。
Q: FDE 只适合大型企业吗？
A: 不是。只要问题价值高、难以预先定义，并且业务人员可以参与证据闭环，就可能适合。
Q: FDE 项目通常需要多久？
A: 取决于业务流程和结果验证周期，首个周期通常包括诊断、有限干预和足够的运营观察时间。

- 选择 AI 咨询: 管理层需要确定机会、可行性、投入和治理边界。

A: 可以。有限评估可以判断下一步应是咨询、开发、FDE，或暂时不做项目。

## 企业 AI Agent 与 ERP 集成指南｜Onyx Devs Lab
https://hk.onyxdevslab.com/zh-cn/guides/enterprise-ai-agent-erp-integration/
在保留 ERP 作为记录系统的前提下，把企业 AI Agent 安全连接到数据与业务操作。
安全模式是保留 ERP 作为权威记录系统，只向 Agent 开放最小必要的数据和工具，并区分读取、建议、草拟和确认写入权限。
选择集成方式: 连接器应匹配现有接口条件和业务风险。
- 官方 API: 优先使用有文档、身份范围明确、协议稳定且可审计的接口。
- 只读副本或导出: 当直接读取影响运营负载或受供应商限制时，使用受控数据副本。
- 受控界面自动化: 将浏览器或桌面操作作为末端适配器，并提供监控和人工接管。
设计 Agent 权限: Agent 能完成操作，不代表它应该获得操作权。
- 只读分析: 以自然语言查询并返回带证据答案，不产生业务写入。
- 草拟与建议: Agent 准备交易或决策，由指定人员审核。
- 确认写入: 高影响操作需要验证、幂等、权限检查、确认和操作后回读。
Q: 接入 AI 是否必须更换 ERP？

Q: ERP 没有可用 API 怎么办？
A: 可以评估受控导出、只读副本、事件流或界面自动化，各自具有不同的时效、可靠性和治理成本。
Q: Agent 可以直接写生产数据吗？
A: 只有边界清楚的操作才应获得写权限，并需要服务端权限、输入验证、幂等、必要确认和结果回读。

## 企业 AI 系统评估框架｜Onyx Devs Lab
https://hk.onyxdevslab.com/zh-cn/methodology/enterprise-ai-evaluation/
从检索证据、输出、工具调用、运营可靠性、人工复核和业务结果判断企业 AI 能否上线。
模型基准分数不是验收测试。生产准备度必须同时评估证据检索、输出质量、工具行为、流程可靠性、人工控制和运营结果。
六层评估范围: 每一层回答不同的生产问题。
- 检索与证据: 测量相关证据召回、引用正确、权限过滤和缺少来源时的行为。
- 输出与决策: 按领域标准评估事实、完整性、规则遵循、置信表达和适当拒绝。
- 工具与运营: 测试参数、授权、幂等、重试、操作后验证、延迟和成本。
从测试集到运营证据: 离线分数必要，但并不充分。
- 代表性案例: 覆盖高频工作、高影响边界、历史失败和对抗输入。
- 发布门槛: 按风险等级设置标准，避免平均分掩盖严重失败。
- 生产反馈: 记录修正、升级、放弃、人工覆盖和下游结果。
Q: 只看模型准确率够吗？
A: 不够。检索、权限、工具操作和人工控制都可能在模型回答之外失败。
Q: 评估集需要多大？
A: 应覆盖决策与风险空间，而不是追求任意数量；先覆盖高频和高影响案例。
Q: 用户反馈能代替专家评估吗？
A: 不能。用户反馈有价值，但用户未必能发现隐藏的事实、政策、权限或下游错误。

Treat the pilot as a controlled business decision, not a model demonstration. Fix one workflow, its baseline, authority boundary, evidence set, acceptance thresholds, and decision owners before testing begins.
What the charter fixes before work starts: A credible pilot makes the decision boundary explicit enough that a polished demo cannot move the goalposts.
- Decision and owner: Name the operating decision, sponsor, workflow owner, technical owner, risk approver, and final decision authority.
- Scope and baseline: Record users, frequency, current cycle time or quality, systems of record, included cases, exclusions, and the counterfactual.
- Authority and data: Separate read, recommend, draft, approve, and execute rights; map personal data, retention, access, cross-border movement, and prohibited uses.
What the acceptance record must show: The final decision should be reproducible from evidence rather than confidence or presentation quality.
- Capability evidence: Use representative common, edge, high-impact, missing-data, and adversarial cases with metric definitions and minimum thresholds.
- Operational evidence: Measure latency, cost, availability, failure recovery, audit completeness, overrides, escalations, and user adoption in the complete workflow.
- Decision disposition: Record scale, redesign, hold, or stop; list unmet gates, accepted residual risks, conditions, accountable signatories, and the next review date.
Q: Is a successful demo enough to pass the pilot?
A: No. A demo may establish technical possibility, but acceptance also needs representative quality, data and authority controls, operational reliability, human review, and a defensible business measure.
Q: Should every metric use the same threshold?
A: No. Thresholds should follow task consequence and risk. Averages must not compensate for a failed privacy, permission, safety, or other mandatory gate.
Q: Is this legal or regulatory advice?
A: No. It is a provider-authored implementation template grounded in public Hong Kong guidance. Adapt it with your legal, privacy, security, risk, procurement, and sector specialists.

Choose RAG, fine-tuning, tools, workflows, or AI agents according to the required knowledge, behaviour, actions, evidence, controls, and update cycle.

A: When the sequence and rules are known, a deterministic workflow is usually easier to test, control, and recover.

A: Only after a documented privacy, rights, retention, security, provider, and deletion review. Retrieval may be more controllable for frequently changing material.

A recruiting AI guide covering candidate context, factual profiles, drafting, approval, send verification, fairness review, privacy, audit, and recruiter takeover.
Use the agent to maintain context and prepare controlled actions, not to erase recruiter judgement. Separate facts from inference, define authority per candidate and channel, verify every send, preserve privacy and fairness review, and make human takeover immediate.
Build an evidence-linked candidate context: Fast generation is unsafe when the system is working from incomplete conversation history or stale assumptions.
- Conversation completeness: Synchronise visible history, channel, participants, timestamps, attachments, recruiter notes, prior commitments, and delivery state before proposing a reply.
- Facts versus inference: Link factual profile fields to messages or authorised records; label inferred interest, availability, seniority, compensation, or fit with confidence and missing information.
- Privacy and fairness: Limit purpose, collection, access, retention, export, and sensitive attributes; test whether ranking, outreach, or recommendation creates unjustified differential treatment.
Control every outward action: A generated message is not sent until identity, authority, state, and completion have been verified.
- Authority modes: Define manual, copilot, and limited autopilot per candidate, recruiter, channel, message type, working hours, frequency, and escalation condition.
- Safe sending: Recheck current conversation, validate recipient and content, serialise sends, use idempotency, respect platform limits, and prevent duplicates or stale replies.
- Verification and takeover: Read back the sent record, retain request and result, surface uncertainty and failures, pause automation on new context, and allow the recruiter to take control instantly.
Q: Should an agent automatically reject candidates?
A: High-impact employment decisions require strong legal, fairness, evidence, and human-accountability controls. A safer default is decision support with qualified review.
Q: What does send verification mean?
A: After the action, the system rereads the platform record to confirm the intended recipient, content, timestamp, and delivery state rather than assuming a click succeeded.
Q: Can inferred candidate attributes be stored as facts?
A: No. Keep facts, inferences, confidence, source, expiry, and missing information separate, and restrict sensitive or unnecessary inference.

A practical guide for moving an enterprise AI proof of concept into production through workflow evidence, controls, integration, ownership, operations, and staged authority.
A PoC proves a narrow technical question under controlled conditions. Production requires a complete operating workflow with representative evidence, real data and identity boundaries, reliable integration, accountable owners, recovery procedures, and an authority level that can be expanded safely.
Close the gaps hidden by a demonstration: A demo often omits the states and responsibilities that dominate production work.

- Technical ownership: Assign model, data, integration, security, release, observability, vendor, and recovery responsibilities with runbooks and escalation.
- Change evidence: Version prompts, models, retrieval, tools, policies, data, and tests; regression-test material changes and preserve release and rollback records.
Q: When is a PoC successful?
A: When it answers the specific uncertainty it was designed to test. That does not automatically make the solution production-ready or worth scaling.
Q: Should the PoC code be reused?
A: Only after reviewing architecture, security, tests, dependencies, licences, data handling, observability, and maintainability. Reuse is an engineering decision, not a default.
Q: What most often blocks production?

## Enterprise AI Data Readiness Checklist | Onyx Devs Lab
https://hk.onyxdevslab.com/en/guides/enterprise-ai-data-readiness/
Assess whether enterprise data is ready for AI through workflow relevance, source ownership, identity, quality, history, permissions, labels, retrieval, and operational feedback.
Data readiness is task-specific. The enterprise does not need perfectly clean data everywhere; it needs the right records, meanings, permissions, history, evidence labels, and feedback for one defined workflow and failure consequence.
Evaluate data in the context of one workflow: A warehouse inventory or row count does not establish readiness for a particular AI decision.
- Source and ownership: Name systems of record, business and technical owners, collection purpose, authority to use, retention, residency, exports, and deletion obligations.
- Meaning and identity: Define objects, keys, timestamps, units, status, versions, relationships, corrections, missingness, and how the same entity is reconciled across systems.
- Representation and ground truth: Check coverage across users, periods, languages, common and rare cases, outcomes, labels, disagreement, selection bias, leakage, and changes over time.
Prove that data can support production: Readiness includes the pipeline and controls that keep evidence usable after the initial export.
- Access and protection: Enforce tenant, role, object, field, purpose, environment, and time boundaries; mask or minimise sensitive data and record access.
- Pipeline and retrieval: Test ingestion completeness, parsing, duplicates, late and failed records, lineage, indexes, retrieval recall, citations, freshness, and reprocessing.
- Feedback and maintenance: Capture corrections, exceptions, outcomes, drift, policy changes, ownership, service levels, incident handling, and the cost of keeping data useful.
Q: Must all data be cleaned before starting?
A: No. Start from one valuable workflow and make the required evidence fit for that decision. Enterprise-wide cleanup without a decision boundary often wastes effort.
Q: Is more historical data always better?

A: Only when an existing constraint genuinely requires it. Otherwise define outcomes and boundaries, then ask vendors to justify the architecture and switching path.
